I have a complicated relationship with ads.

I understand why websites have them. I understand why free apps have them. I even understand why that free game I played for five minutes wants to show me an advertisement every time I finish a level.

But there is a difference between seeing an occasional advertisement and having your phone constantly trying to sell you something.

Ads appear inside websites, apps, games, and sometimes in places where you really did not ask for them. And behind many of those ads are trackers, analytics systems, and third-party services quietly making network requests in the background.

For years, blocking ads on Android meant, using a dedicated ad-blocking app, setting up a VPN-based blocker, or going down the rabbit hole of rooting your phone.

It turns out there is a much simpler option.

Your Android phone already has a feature called Private DNS.

And with the right DNS provider, that little setting can block a surprising amount of advertising and tracking across your device, without root, without modifying system files, and without installing an ad-blocking app.

So, how much of Android's advertising can you actually eliminate without rooting your phone?

Let's find out.
